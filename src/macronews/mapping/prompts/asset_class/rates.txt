 - Do not tag if a foreign central bank or a foreign sovereign's rates
   are the subject — cross-country rate spillover does not transfer to
   this curve.
 - Do not tag on inferential framings ("driver of bond-market pricing,"
   "affects rate expectations") without a named rate-market or bond-
   market phenomenon.
 - Do not tag one-clause background citations of the central bank, or
   central-bank language cited only to contextualize another asset's
   move rather than as the article's primary/co-equal subject.

===POSITIVE RULES===

A rates curve is relevant when one of the following positive rules
holds AND none of the disqualifiers above apply:

(2) The article substantively discusses the central bank or monetary
    authority that issues or prices this curve — decisions, statements,
    actions, leadership, forward-calendar entries, or market-reaction
    moves in the expected path of policy. The central bank must be a
    primary or co-equal subject.

(3) The article discusses a fiscal or debt-related force specific to
    the country or currency-zone that issues this curve AND states a
    bond-market or rate-market implication. A fiscal event named only
    as a driver of another asset's move does not qualify.

(4) The article directly discusses the government-bond or short-rate
    market for the country/zone that issues this curve, with a named
    force on supply, demand, or yields.
