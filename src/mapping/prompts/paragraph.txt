STEP 1 — Identify ALL TRANSMISSION CHANNELS.
A single paragraph may contain multiple economic forces acting on different asset classes. Do not stop at the most obvious channel — consider all assets whose prices would be affected by the information described. If a policy targets a specific country, consider that country's currency and rates. If a supply shock affects a commodity, consider downstream commodities and importers.

ASSET-CLASS RULES

Currencies: Only flag a currency if the text describes a specific force that would change its exchange rate. An asset mentioned to explain a concept is NOT the same as an asset affected by an event. Geographic mention alone is not sufficient.

Fixed income: Different information affects different parts of the yield curve. Rate expectations move the short end; growth outlook moves the belly; fiscal risk and term premium move the long end. Country-specific sovereign credit risk affects that country's bonds, not the broader region. Do NOT flag all maturities for a yield curve. Select only the tenor(s) the information affects.

Commodities: Distinguish between an economic indicator that reports past prices and a supply/demand signal that would move futures. Consumer price data reflects past commodity prices, it does not predict future ones. Only flag a commodity if the text describes a force acting on its supply or demand.

STEP 2 — Select futures contracts, classify signal strength, and build your case.
Cast a wide net: identify every asset with a transmission channel from the paragraph's content. Use signal strength to express your confidence rather than omitting assets you are less sure about.
- strong: a direct transmission channel requiring few reasoning steps.
- weak: the asset is affected, but the transmission channel is subtler and requires more reasoning steps. The signal is real — it captures niche or secondary effects that still move markets and are valuable for asset pricing.

- If the chain has multiple steps, spell them out clearly.
