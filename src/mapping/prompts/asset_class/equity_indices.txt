These rules apply to broad country-level or region-level equity
indices. Under general rule (1), the index must be named directly — a
constituent company's name does not count as the index's name.

  - The index named only as a pricing unit or benchmark for a single
    company's stock move, including automated stock-performance
    templates.
  - Incidental constituent mentions — added-to-index events, peer
    comparisons, exchange-disclosure footnotes, conference topic lists.
  - Monetary policy news about a country without an equity-market
    transmission channel stated.
  - Regional macro discussion (e.g., eurozone) does not qualify
    each member country's national index. Only the regional index
    qualifies; country-level indices require macro discussion
    specific to that country.

===POSITIVE RULES===

An index is relevant when one of the following positive rules holds AND
none of the disqualifiers above apply:

(2) A macroeconomic or policy force with a clear impact on the country
    or region's equity market is a substantive subject of at least one
    paragraph. The force must be discussed at this index's level of
    jurisdiction: a pan-region force (e.g., eurozone) qualifies the
    pan-region index, not any member country's national index.
    For example, if the story is about the Eurozone economy, the 
    Euro Stoxx 50 Index is the primary vehicle for the story, not 
    France's CAC 40 Index, Germany's DAX Index, or Italy's FTSE MIB Index.
    One-clause background citations do not qualify.

(3) A company whose operations or market weight make its news carry
    clear index-wide implications — not merely sector-wide — is the
    subject of the article. The article must frame the event as
    broad-market or index-level news, not just single-company news;
    the index-level read-through must be explicit in the article,
    even when the company is a major index constituent.
