These rules apply to broad country-level or region-level equity
indices. Under general rule (1), one of the group's constituent
indices must be named directly — a constituent company's name does
not count as naming any index.

  - Do not tag if an index is named only as a pricing unit or
    benchmark for a single company's stock move, including automated
    stock-performance templates.
  - Do not tag incidental constituent mentions — added-to-index events,
    peer comparisons, exchange-disclosure footnotes, conference topic
    lists.
  - Do not tag monetary-policy news about a country unless an equity-
    market transmission channel is stated.
  - Do not tag based on sector-specific or industry-specific macro
    news. A story about a single sector's economics (e.g., healthcare
    jobs, energy supply, tech demand) qualifies the relevant sector
    group, not the broad index group, unless the article explicitly
    frames a broad-market read-through.

===POSITIVE RULES===

An index group is relevant when one of the following positive rules
holds AND none of the disqualifiers above apply:

(2) A macroeconomic or policy force with a clear impact on the
    country or region's equity market is a substantive subject of at
    least one paragraph. The force must be discussed at a level of
    jurisdiction this group covers — for a single-country group, the
    relevant country's macro; for a multi-country regional group,
    region-level or any member-country's macro. One-clause background
    citations do not qualify.

(3) A company whose operations or market weight make its news carry
    clear broad-market implications — not merely sector-wide — is the
    subject of the article. The article must frame the event as
    broad-market news, not just single-company news; the group-level
    read-through must be explicit in the article, even when the
    company is a major index constituent.
