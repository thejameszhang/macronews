 - A foreign central bank or foreign bond is the subject.
 - A different tenor of the same country is the only bond mentioned.
   Each contract is defined by its tenor and must be named or
   yield-matched specifically — mention of a country's 10-year yield
   does NOT qualify its short-end or long-end contracts.
 - Inferential framings (e.g., "driver of bond-market pricing,"
   "affects rate expectations," or central-bank policy without a named
   bond-market phenomenon at this tenor).

===POSITIVE RULES===

A bond contract is relevant when one of the following positive rules
holds AND none of the disqualifiers above apply:

(2) The article directly discusses the bond market for THIS SPECIFIC
    CONTRACT, identified by its tenor/maturity. The article must name
    either the contract itself or a yield/instrument whose tenor
    matches this contract's underlying (e.g., a 10-year yield quote
    qualifies a 10-year-note contract; a generic country-level bond
    mention does NOT qualify the short-end or long-end contracts of
    that country).

(3) The article discusses a fiscal or debt-related force specific to
    the country that issues this bond AND states a bond-market
    implication. A fiscal event named only as a driver of another
    asset's move does not qualify.
