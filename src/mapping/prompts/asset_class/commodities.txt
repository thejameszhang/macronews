 - A commodity used as a pricing unit, reserve asset, or denominator
   with no force acting on it (e.g., "gold-denominated" is not a force
   on gold).
 - Inflation or monetary-policy discussion without the commodity named
   as a primary subject.
 - Vertical production-chain relationships where the article names
   only the raw/upstream commodity. A crude-oil article does NOT
   extend to Heating Oil, RBOB Gasoline, or Gas Oil; a soybean article
   does NOT extend to Soybean Oil or Soybean Meal. The refined/
   downstream product or a force specifically acting on its market
   must be named in the article.
 - Background mentions. If the commodity or the force acting on it
   appears only in a parenthetical, footnote, or one-clause aside
   (e.g., "the report predates the Iran conflict, which has sent
   energy prices sharply higher"), the commodity is not a primary
   or co-equal subject and Rule 2 is not satisfied.
 - Corporate events that incidentally name the commodity. Mergers,
   acquisitions, earnings, capex plans, or portfolio-composition
   language ("the company produces gold and copper") do not
   qualify unless the article describes a commodity-market impact
   of the event.

===POSITIVE RULES===

A commodity is relevant when one of the following positive rules holds
AND none of the disqualifiers above apply:

(2) The article's primary or co-equal subject is the commodity this
    contract trades, or a specific force acting on it — a supply or
    demand event, producing region or facility, geopolitical or
    regulatory action, weather event, sanctions, or industry
    organization decision (OPEC, IEA, USDA). When the article names a
    generic label covering multiple graded contracts (e.g., "oil"),
    all primary vehicles for that label qualify; when it names a
    specific grade, only that grade qualifies.

(3) The article names a commodity or commodity-specific event as a
    primary or co-equal subject, and this contract is a different
    grade, delivery location, or origin of the same physical
    commodity (e.g., WTI and Brent are horizontal grades of crude
    oil; LME Copper and COMEX Copper are horizontal delivery
    locations for copper). The substitution applies only when the
    force described would act on this contract through the same
    mechanism. Distinct commodities in the same category do not
    qualify.
