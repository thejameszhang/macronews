 - Do not tag if the commodity is used only as a pricing unit, reserve
   asset, or denominator, with no force acting on it ("gold-denominated"
   is not a force on gold).
 - Do not tag inflation or monetary-policy discussion unless the
   commodity is named as a primary subject.
 - Do not tag the refined/downstream product based only on a raw/
   upstream-commodity article. A crude-oil article does not extend to
   Heating Oil, RBOB Gasoline, or Gas Oil; a soybean article does not
   extend to Soybean Oil or Soybean Meal. The downstream product or a
   force specifically acting on its market must be named.
 - Do not tag if the commodity or the force acting on it appears only
   in a parenthetical, footnote, or one-clause aside ("the report
   predates the Iran conflict, which has sent energy prices sharply
   higher"). Rule (2) requires the commodity be a primary or co-equal
   subject.
 - Do not tag corporate events that only incidentally name the
   commodity — mergers, acquisitions, earnings, capex plans, portfolio-
   composition language ("the company produces gold and copper"). The
   article must describe a commodity-market impact of the event.

===POSITIVE RULES===

A commodity is relevant when one of the following positive rules holds
AND none of the disqualifiers above apply:

(2) The article's primary or co-equal subject is the commodity this
    contract trades, or a specific force acting on it — a supply or
    demand event, producing region or facility, geopolitical or
    regulatory action, weather event, sanctions, or industry
    organization decision (OPEC, IEA, USDA). When the article names a
    generic label covering multiple graded contracts (e.g., "oil"),
    all primary vehicles for that label qualify; when it names a
    specific grade, only that grade qualifies.

(3) The article names a commodity or commodity-specific event as a
    primary or co-equal subject, and this contract is a different
    grade, delivery location, or origin of the same physical
    commodity (e.g., WTI and Brent are horizontal grades of crude
    oil; LME Copper and COMEX Copper are horizontal delivery
    locations for copper). The substitution applies only when the
    force described would act on this contract through the same
    mechanism. Distinct commodities in the same category do not
    qualify.
