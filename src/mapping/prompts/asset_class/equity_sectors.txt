These rules apply to US-listed GICS sector ETFs. Under general rule
(1), the sector must be named directly — a constituent company's
name does not count as the sector's name.

  - Foreign-market sectors, unless the article explicitly states a
    transmission channel to the US sector.
  - Incidental constituent mentions — peer comparisons, conference
    topic lists, stock-move footers, analyst-target boilerplate.
  - Macro rate or policy news without a sector-specific transmission
    channel stated in the text.
  - Corporate events that incidentally name the sector's commodities
    or constituents. Mergers, acquisitions, earnings, capex plans, or
    portfolio descriptions do not qualify unless the article describes
    a sector-wide impact of the event.

===POSITIVE RULES===

A sector is relevant when one of the following positive rules holds AND
none of the disqualifiers above apply:

(2) A commodity, regulation, or demand/supply shock central to the
    sector is the primary or co-equal subject of the article. The
    link must be direct; the commodity or regulation cannot be a
    minor slice of the sector's activity, and cannot appear only as
    a sub-component of a broader story. For example, a story about 
    gold and silver prices increasing alone does not warrant a mapping 
    for the Materials sector. One-clause background citations do not
    qualify.

(3) A US bellwether company — one of a handful of dominant constituents
    driving a large share of the sector's weight — is the subject of
    news with clear sector-wide implications.
